# Episode 1: The Black Ocean
## Full Novel-Length Chapter

## Part 1: The Last Breath

The rescue boat cut through the flooded streets of San Francisco like a knife through water—because that's all the streets were now. Water. Everything was water.

Aren gripped the edge of the metal hull, his knuckles white against the rust-stained steel. Around him, ten other members of the Emergency Response Team checked their equipment for the hundredth time. Rope. Harnesses. Flares. Weapons that probably wouldn't do anything against *them*, but humans liked pretending they had a chance.

Below the waterline, two blocks down, there were people in the submerged Central Tower. Families. Children. They'd radioed for help three days ago before the communication network collapsed.

That was six days ago.

Nobody expected the rescue mission to succeed. But Aren's commander, General Thorn, had ordered it anyway. "We show them we haven't given up," he'd said, his scarred face impassive. "Even if it's the last thing we do."

Aren was seventeen. He'd been a regular high school student before the portals opened. He'd worried about calculus tests and whether Jessica Chen might go to prom with him.

Now he worried about whether his oxygen tank would last long enough to find people who were probably already dead.

"Aren!" His team lead, Officer Maria Santos, called from the wheelhouse. "Depth reading?"

Aren checked the sonar display. The water had risen so much that the old city was now a dark maze of submerged buildings, twisted metal, and things that *moved* beneath the surface. Things with too many eyes.

"Forty meters to the tower," Aren replied, his voice steady. He'd trained himself to keep the fear out of his voice. "No major movement detected on the last sweep."

*That's a lie,* he thought. The sonar showed at least three massive shapes circling the zone. They moved like sharks. They probably *were* sharks. Mutated ones, warped by whatever mana was—the energy that had flooded the world when the portals opened.

Maria nodded, her weathered face tight. She'd been a police officer for twenty years. The apocalypse had made her older. "Suit up. We go in five minutes."

Aren pulled on his diving suit—reinforced with some kind of experimental mana-resistant material that probably cost more than his parents' entire house. The fabric felt strange against his skin, almost alive, humming at a frequency just below hearing.

One of the younger recruits, a kid named Danny who couldn't have been more than twenty, was shaking.

"First dive?" Aren asked.

Danny nodded, not trusting his voice.

"You'll be fine," Aren said. It was probably a lie. Seventeen out of the first rescue team's eighteen members hadn't come back. The one survivor had spent two days screaming before they'd sedated him.

But lies were all they had left.

## Part 2: Into the Deep

The water was black.

Not dark. Not shadowy. *Black.* Like looking into an oil slick mixed with ink. Aren's dive light carved a weak tunnel through it, illuminating particles of sediment and things he preferred not to identify.

Mana-corrupted water, the scientists said. Changed on a molecular level. It didn't taste like salt anymore if you accidentally got some in your mouth—it tasted like electricity and copper and something that made your teeth ache.

Aren swam in formation with five others. Maria was point, Danny was trailing, and two veteran divers—Garcia and Hsu—flanked them. Their sonar showed they were twenty meters down, approaching the tower zone.

The buildings appeared out of the darkness like the ribs of some colossal skeleton. Cars hung suspended in the water, caught in invisible currents. A child's playground slide spiraled down from a building entrance. A red bicycle lay on the street. Aren tried not to think about who it had belonged to.

His oxygen regulators hissed with each breath. Forty minutes of air. Then they had to surface or die.

Fifteen minutes to the building. Fifteen minutes to search. Fifteen minutes back up. They'd get maybe five minutes inside if they were lucky.

*Not lucky,* something whispered at the edge of Aren's consciousness. *Never lucky anymore.*

They reached the tower entrance—a gaping mouth of darkness that no light could fully penetrate. The automatic doors hung open, twisted by water pressure.

Maria signaled: Stay close. Stay alert.

They entered.

The lobby was a graveyard of everyday objects transformed into monuments. A reception desk tilted at a 45-degree angle. Potted plants rotted in corners. There were bones too. Aren forced himself to keep moving.

The emergency stairs led upward. They followed them, swimming through corridors that should have held air but held only the suffocating pressure of dark water.

Aren's light caught something—a reflection. Eyes. *Many* eyes.

All around them, suspended in the water like a living ceiling, were jellyfish. Except jellyfish didn't have barbed spines that glowed with bioluminescent purple. Jellyfish didn't move with *intelligence*.

Garcia grabbed Aren's arm—hard. *Danger.*

The creatures descended.

There was no time to think. Aren's training took over. He pulled his spear gun—not a weapon, really, more of a last resort—and fired. The barbed spear passed through the creature's gelatinous body and came out the other side, useless.

The jellyfish didn't even slow down.

Maria threw a device that erupted in a cloud of chemicals—a deterrent. The creatures shrieked—an actual sound, a piercing wail that traveled through the water directly into Aren's skull—and dispersed, but only slightly. They were circling now, hunting.

*We're in their territory,* Aren realized. *We're prey.*

Then something massive moved in the darkness below.

Aren looked down and saw rows of teeth. Sharp. Arranged in a circle. Growing larger as the creature rose.

A shark. Mutated beyond recognition. Its skin rippled with patterns of bioluminescence—blues and greens that painted the water with an alien light. Its fins were serrated. Its eyes were golden and absolutely, completely intelligent.

It wasn't hunting on instinct. It was hunting on *purpose*.

Maria made a decision. She signaled frantically: *Abort. Return to surface.*

They turned. Fast. But not fast enough.

The creature moved like it was born in the water—which, Aren supposed, it was. It moved like it was the water. It moved like this was its world and they were just temporary visitors soon to be evicted.

Permanently.

Danny screamed. A cloud of red bloomed in the water where he'd been. The young recruit had lasted approximately four seconds into actual combat.

*Not good odds,* Aren thought with the strange clarity of impending death.

The shark circled back for another pass. Garcia tried to shoot it with some kind of explosive spear. The creature flicked its tail, and the force of water displacement threw Garcia backward like he weighed nothing. The diver crashed into a wall twenty meters away and didn't get up.

Hsu was already swimming, abandoning them. Aren didn't blame him.

Maria turned to face the creature, knife drawn—a useless gesture against something that could bite through steel. She gestured to Aren.

*Go.*

Aren shook his head.

*GO!*

The shark came again, this time for Maria. Aren saw his team lead's eyes widen as she understood she was about to die. She'd known it was possible. Intellectually, all of them had. But knowing and *understanding* are different things.

Something broke in Aren. Or maybe something was finally assembled correctly, after a lifetime of small, mundane moments that had led to this exact second.

He swam toward the shark.

Not away. Toward.

It was the stupid decision. The decision that got you killed. But Aren had spent his entire life being safe, being careful, being the kind of person who colored inside the lines and never made waves.

Maybe it was time to be someone else.

Aren angled his body to intercept the shark's path. Behind him, Maria shouted something into her comm unit. Probably calling for him to get back. Probably already grieving.

The shark's jaws opened.

Aren could see the serrated edges of its teeth, each one the size of his hand. He could see the darkness of its throat. He could smell something ancient and hungry.

He drove his spear directly into the creature's left eye.

The shark shrieked—that piercing wail again—and thrashed. Its tail whipped around. Aren felt impact, heard something in his suit tear, felt cold water seeping in. His regulator sparked. His oxygen flow stuttered.

*That's it,* he thought distantly. *That's how it ends.*

The shark turned on him fully now, forgetting about Maria. Its jaws came down.

Aren couldn't move. His left arm was useless—broken or crushed, he couldn't tell anymore. The water around him was turning red. His vision was getting dark at the edges.

But he looked into that one remaining golden eye and smiled.

*At least I tried,* he thought.

The jaws closed.

## Part 3: Between Worlds

Death tasted like static and electricity.

Aren's last conscious thought was confusion—not pain, which he'd expected, but a strange tingling sensation, like every nerve in his body was vibrating at a frequency just outside reality.

*This is it,* he thought. *This is what dying feels like.*

Darkness swallowed him.

And then, from somewhere beyond the darkness, a voice spoke.

It wasn't a sound. It was something deeper. Something that bypassed his ears entirely and spoke directly to whatever remained of his consciousness.

"SYSTEM INITIALIZATION DETECTED."

Aren tried to respond, but he had no mouth. He had no body. He was only thought now, tumbling through an infinite void.

"SUBJECT: HUMAN CONSCIOUSNESS (DESIGNATION: AREN)"

*I'm dead,* Aren thought. *This is my brain dying. This is neurons firing one last time.*

"CONSCIOUSNESS INTEGRITY: OPTIMAL"  
"MANA SATURATION: CRITICAL"  
"DIMENSIONAL STABILITY: COMPROMISED"

The voice sounded almost confused. Aren found that strange. Voices—or whatever this was—didn't usually sound confused when you were dying.

"ANOMALY DETECTED: INFINITE DEVOUR SYSTEM [CORRUPTED]"

*What?*

"INSTALLATION PARAMETERS: NON-STANDARD"  
"SAFETY LIMITERS: OFFLINE"  
"MORAL CONSTRAINTS: OFFLINE"  
"SURVIVAL PROTOCOLS: EMERGENCY MODE ENGAGED"

Aren wanted to ask what was happening, but the void was pulling him deeper. He could feel himself scattering, like a piece of paper being torn into smaller and smaller pieces.

"HOST CURRENTLY INCOMPATIBLE WITH HUMAN FORM"  
"INITIATING EMERGENCY TRANSMUTATION"

And then—

Pain.

Real, vivid, absolute pain.

Aren screamed, but no sound came out. His body was changing. He could feel it, bone restructuring, organs rearranging, his entire genetic code being rewritten by forces he didn't understand and couldn't fight.

It felt like drowning.

It felt like burning.

It felt like every single cell in his body was being disassembled and put back together wrong.

And then, as suddenly as it had begun, the pain stopped.

Aren floated in the darkness, breathing—*wait, breathing?*—and tried to understand what had happened.

His lungs pulled in water. Oxygen-rich water. His body processed it without choking. His heart beat in a rhythm that felt natural, if alien.

*I'm still alive,* he realized. *Somehow, impossibly, I'm still alive.*

The voice spoke again.

"TRANSMUTATION COMPLETE"  
"NEW FORM: BOTTLENOSE DOLPHIN (TURSIOPS TRUNCATUS)"  
"BEGINNING CONSCIOUSNESS INTEGRATION PROCEDURE"  
"WARNING: NEURAL RESTRUCTURING IN PROGRESS"

Aren opened his eyes.

## Part 4: Awakening

The world was different.

Sound came first. A sonar pulse that painted the water in images Aren could understand directly, without his eyes having to process it. He could sense the shape of the sunken building around him—the echoes told him more than sight ever could.

He could taste the water. *Really* taste it. Chemicals, minerals, the presence of other living things creating patterns in his consciousness like scent mixed with sound mixed with something that had no human equivalent.

His body was wrong. Impossibly wrong.

Aren looked down at his flippers and had a moment of primal panic. *No. No, this is wrong. This is very wrong.*

"CONSCIOUSNESS INTEGRATION: 75% COMPLETE"

*Get me out,* Aren tried to think. *Get me back. I want my body. I want to go home.*

But there was no home anymore. Home was underwater. Home was in ruins.

Aren dove deeper into that particular rabbit hole of existential horror, because what else was he going to do? His brain was still mostly human, but his body was definitely, absolutely not.

The integration continued. Aren's human panic slowly gave way to something else. Acceptance, maybe. Or shock. Or both.

He could move. That was surprising. His dolphin body knew how to move—it came with knowledge built in, like instinct, but more precise. He flexed his flippers, and his body responded perfectly. He arced through the water in a way that felt almost natural.

"CONSCIOUSNESS INTEGRATION: 95% COMPLETE"

The shark was still here.

Aren's new sonar sense painted it in vivid acoustic detail: wounded, circling slowly, the impact of the spear having destroyed one eye. It was above him, maybe fifteen meters up, its bioluminescent patterns flickering with agitation.

Hungry. The shark was definitely still hungry.

*Can't have that,* Aren thought, and was alarmed at how *cold* that thought felt.

The system responded.

"DEVOUR SYSTEM ACTIVATED"  
"SCANNING TARGET: MUTANT PREDATOR SHARK (SPECIES: CORRUPTED GREAT WHITE HYBRID)"  
"POWER LEVEL: UNCOMMON+ (EVOLVED)"  
"DEVOURING PROCESS: AVAILABLE"

*Devour?* Aren tried to understand. *What does that—*

The system didn't wait for him to finish the thought.

Before Aren could process what was happening, his dolphin body moved on its own. He accelerated upward, faster than anything that size should be able to move, faster than physics had any right to allow.

The shark noticed too late.

Aren's teeth—and *holy God* he had teeth, so many teeth, teeth like needles arranged in perfect curves—closed around the creature's injured eye socket.

The shark thrashed, but Aren didn't let go. He bit deeper, and something broke inside him. Or maybe something unlocked.

He ate.

And as he ate, something extraordinary happened.

The shark's knowledge flooded into him. Not pictures or words, but *understanding*. He knew how to hunt like this thing hunted. He knew how to move through the water the way it moved. He knew its senses, its instincts, its perfect predatory knowledge.

He knew it had been a great white shark once, before mana had warped it. It had been beautiful, before. Fast. Efficient. Now it was merely efficient.

*EXCELLENT,* the system's voice said, and Aren could swear it sounded pleased.

When it was over, there was nothing left but sediment and a fading taste of blood.

Aren floated in the water, breathing, processing, and tried very hard not to scream.

"DEVOUR COMPLETE"  
"TRAITS ABSORBED:"  
"+SHARP TEETH: Attack damage increased 15%"  
"+PREDATORY INSTINCT: Hunting success rate increased 20%"  
"+WATER MASTERY: Swimming speed increased 20%"  
"+HUNTING SENSITIVITY: Echolocation range increased 30%"  
"+IRON SKIN: Defense increased 10%"

"EVOLUTION POINTS GAINED: 5"  
"TOTAL EVOLUTION POINTS: 5/100 TO NEXT STAGE"

Aren wanted to be horrified. Wanted to feel sick. His humanity—his *real* humanity—should have been screaming in protest.

Instead, he felt... better.

His senses had expanded. His body felt strong in a way human strength could never match. The water around him wasn't an obstacle anymore; it was home. It was possibility.

*This is what the shark felt,* he realized. *This is what it's like to be a predator. To be at the top of the food chain.*

And God help him, it felt *good*.

"CONSCIOUSNESS INTEGRATION: 100% COMPLETE"  
"SYSTEM STATUS: ONLINE"  
"WELCOME, AREN. YOUR JOURNEY BEGINS NOW."

Aren oriented himself using his new sonar sense. The sunken tower was nearby. The water was still black. There were still things moving in the darkness.

But Aren wasn't afraid anymore.

He had teeth. He had speed. He had power coursing through every fiber of his being.

He had the Infinite Devour System.

And for the first time since the apocalypse began, Aren felt like he might actually survive.

The question was: what would he become in the process?

## Part 5: Echoes

It took Aren an hour to find the bodies.

Maria was easy. She hadn't made it far—shock and blood loss had probably taken her within minutes of the attack. Aren recognized her uniform, the ID number on her chest.

He didn't eat her.

The system had tried to suggest it—a helpful little prompt that made him want to scream. The only thing that stopped him was the overwhelming disgust that rose up from whatever remained of his humanity.

*Not humans,* he thought fiercely. *Never humans. I'm not eating people.*

The system didn't argue. Maybe it had limitations. Or maybe it understood that consuming a human would be the thing that finally broke him.

He kept swimming.

Danny's body was scattered across the basement level—there wasn't enough left to identify as human anymore. The shark had been thorough. Aren felt nothing looking at it. That was worse than grief.

Garcia was still alive.

Not well. Not really alive in any meaningful sense. The impact had crushed his ribs, and he was floating unconscious, bleeding into the black water. His oxygen tank read half-full. He had maybe ten minutes before the air ran out and he'd wake up drowning.

Aren approached slowly. The scent-taste of blood filled his new senses, and every predatory instinct in his body sang to just finish it.

He fought those instincts.

It was the hardest thing he'd done since waking up as a dolphin. Harder than accepting the transformation. Harder than understanding that he'd been remade into something that wasn't human.

Aren grabbed Garcia's equipment harness in his jaws and began swimming upward. Not gently—his teeth were designed for shearing through muscle and bone, not for delicate rescue operations. But carefully.

He dragged the wounded man through the water toward the surface.

It took forever. Or maybe just minutes. Time felt strange now, stretched and compressed simultaneously.

The pressure around them decreased. The water lightened. Aren could see the surface, a vague brightness far above.

Garcia's eyes suddenly opened. He saw Aren—saw the shark-headed creature pulling him upward—and his mouth opened to scream.

No sound came out underwater. Just bubbles.

His eyes rolled back. He'd fainted again, probably from shock.

Aren kept swimming.

The rescue boat was still where they'd left it, a metallic dot against the flooded city skyline. Hsu was standing at the railing, soaking wet, frantically waving something at the sky—probably signaling for backup or emergency medical.

When he saw Aren breach the surface, towing Garcia's limp body, he screamed.

"MONSTER! THERE'S A—"

"It's me!" Aren tried to shout back, but dolphins didn't have vocal cords designed for human speech. What came out was a series of clicks and squeaks.

Hsu stopped screaming. He just stared, hand halfway to his rifle.

"Please," Aren tried again, but it still came out as echolocation pulses. "Help him."

Maybe Hsu understood. Or maybe he just saw the unconscious, bleeding diver and the critical nature of the situation overrode his panic. Either way, the veteran quickly lowered a harness and helped pull Garcia onto the boat.

Aren hung in the water, watching, and tried very hard not to be satisfied with how quickly a human could go from hunter to hunted.

"What... what *are* you?" Hsu asked, his weapon raised but not quite aimed.

Aren wanted to answer. Wanted to explain. Wanted to say his name and tell the story of how a regular high school boy died and became something else.

Instead, he made a clicking sound—a dolphin's laugh, or close enough—and dove back underwater.

He had more to hunt.
